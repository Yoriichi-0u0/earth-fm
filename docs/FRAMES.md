# Earth Information Center visual assets

The app bundles NASA / NOAA and Earth Information Center (EIC) visual assets. Their actual retrieval origin is the public repository [`CarsonDavis/compression-comparison`](https://github.com/CarsonDavis/compression-comparison), pinned at commit `2895d06f1a6abc144a5bc74f10daac805e8b0022` and checked on **2026-10-14**. This is an attributed asset mirror, not an official NASA GitHub repository.

The mirror's README says: **“Visual comparison of compression options for earth.gov images”**, explains that the originals came from `public/images/`, and documents the original paths encoded into filenames. It contains `portals/earth-information-center.png`, `visit/immersive-experience/untethered-4.gif`, and other EIC exhibition images. This is the evidence for their EIC relationship. A copy of that README is retained in [`public/frames/MIRROR-README.md`](../public/frames/MIRROR-README.md).

Direct requests to `earth.gov`, `svs.gsfc.nasa.gov`, and NASA EIC endpoints were denied by the development environment's network proxy. We therefore **did not independently compare the mirror's files with current official website bytes**, verify an exact photograph/observation date, or obtain full creator credits. The app labels the assets as an earth.gov mirror and does not invent missing dates or claim a direct NASA download. Original URLs below are reconstructed from the mirror's documented path convention; they are not independently checked live URLs.

Original PNGs and GIF are included unchanged from the mirror. WebP versions are the mirror's quality-80 encodings; the WebM is its VP9 CRF-30 encoding of the GIF. No visual imagery was generated by AI. [`public/frames/provenance.json`](../public/frames/provenance.json) records byte counts, exact raw GitHub retrieval URLs, and SHA-256 hashes for original and compressed versions. [`src/data/frames.ts`](../src/data/frames.ts) supplies display metadata and links. The smaller WebP and WebM versions are available to keep the experience responsive.

## Pairing visual frames and sound

These assets can accompany browser-generated sonification, but they are not the source of the four annual observational tracks. The [NASA GISTEMP, NOAA CO₂/sea-level, and NSIDC ice observations](DATA.md) remain distinct from the visual imagery. Pairing a nighttime image with a temperature melody does not establish that the image measures temperature, that a city has a particular anomaly, or that it was observed in the current playback year.

If the app offers image sonification, brightness, color, and scan position represent the **image's visible pixels**. These are a musical interpretation of the displayed visual, not calibrated radiance, temperature, CO₂, sea level, or ice measurements. Exhibit photographs include walls, people, typography, and displays; all are pixels in the photograph, not environmental observations. Do not conflate the pixel scan with the scientific time-series mode.

Animated imagery should play only through explicit interaction and respect reduced-motion preferences. A paused or static visual is a valid accessible pairing with the generated sound.

## Credits and reuse

Retain the embedded NOAA/NASA credit and the mirror provenance when distributing these visuals. The app is an independent Space Apps project and does not claim NASA or NOAA endorsement. NASA generally permits reuse of its imagery with attribution, but NASA assets may contain third-party material, people, or separately credited work. The mirror supplies no additional asset license or complete production-credit list. Before public distribution beyond this prototype, verify the exact original asset credits and reuse terms through the official EIC site and the [NASA media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).
