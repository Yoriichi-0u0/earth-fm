## Local audition results

The original 5688 JPEG was loaded through **Your Record** in the existing application. This exercised the actual decoder, `useVisualTransport`, `imageToSequence` and `FrameAudio` without introducing a curated record. Use the attribution above with the research evidence. The generic upload UI itself calls the media “Your image,” so it is unsuitable as the final NASA source label.

- **Mapping.** The original JPEG decoded at 1670 × 835. A 384 × 192 canvas produced 48 vertical strips. Average displayed luma spans 0–0.6173 and generates D3 × 9, F3 × 3, G3 × 2, A3 × 3, C4 × 10, D4 × 10 and F4 × 11. RGB weights the harmonics and horizontal position drives stereo pan. The accompanying drone is artistic. The geometry creates two pitch rises with a low gap near strips 21–22. This provides an audible structure to review. Dark margins and unequal globe sizes also affect the notes, so pitch cannot serve as a comparison of degrees Celsius, a Pacific region measurement or an El Niño index.
- **Audio.** Actual Web Audio scheduling produced 144 note oscillators and two drone oscillators. All 48 groups matched the expected base frequency, the two harmonic multiples, their configured detune and 0.5-second spacing. The analyser measured an active signal and zero RMS after the finish and after Stop in the phone viewport. The separate audition is TerraJuke’s stereo PCM WAV export at 120 BPM, 24 kHz, 24.92 seconds, with a −18.69 dBFS peak and −30.07 dBFS RMS. Human musical preference remains to be assessed.
- **Switching and cleanup.** Replacing the still mid-play on desktop and switching to Climate Spiral mid-play in phone emulation closed the old audio context. No new old-source oscillators appeared in the following 700 ms. Both contexts completed with zero page errors and zero outbound HTTP requests during the probe.
- **Mobile resource estimates.** On this Mac, Chromium 156 produced matching strip values at desktop size and in a 390 × 664 iPhone 13 viewport. The one-run file-to-ready times were 465 ms and 882 ms respectively, including automation and UI wait. Twenty repeated canvas reads and strip computations measured 0.4 and 0.5 ms median. A single decoded RGBA still is approximately 5.32 MiB and the analysis buffer is 288 KiB. One 3340 × 1670 RGBA frame would be approximately 21.3 MiB before decoder, GPU and buffering overhead. These are buffer estimates and local timings. Physical iOS/Android playback, battery use, decoder memory and direct remote canvas CORS remain open.
- **Phone presentation.** The 2:1 still fits without horizontal overflow. The transport badges overlap the in-image dates and legend on the phone screenshot. Put credited dates and the legend explanation in readable text outside the media frame before considering a curated view. Supply descriptive text and confirm keyboard and screen-reader behavior.

Compare user interpretation with Climate Spiral Side A before deciding whether this warrants another record.

Recheck visual and audio together if a later review selects the new bytes.
3. **Consider 5687 as a linked companion.** Explain ocean optics alongside the SST picture. Any panel comparison needs explicit selection, retained legends and documented sampling coordinates. Quantitative AVW, SST and sea-level modes each need validated numeric source data and their own units and mapping review.
4. **Use 5620 if the story shifts to sea level.** Start with the ruler and explanation of smoothing. Use the smaller 5603 still for a climate geography comparison if requested. Prefer the dated EIC daily stills over continuous downloading until a timeline use case justifies it.

When the challenge list appears, capture its exact wording and each linked asset, then match source IDs and file digests against this inventory. Check mandatory and optional labels only where the organizer supplies them. Review 5688 against that list before making a competition-source decision.
