## The 85-second demonstration

Let people hear something before explaining everything. The strongest proposed moments are the change of meaning on Flip, personal control through Scratch, and one exact source-to-note reading. These are design judgments from a browser walkthrough, not participant preferences or proof of learning.

| Time | Existing action and visual | Suggested narration | Capture direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 s | Home; select **Climate Spiral**, Side A. | “TerraJuke. Choose an Earth story. Press Play.” | Establish the shelf, selected record and Play. Use opening capture 01. |
| 6–17 s | Press **Play record**. | “First, hear NASA’s Climate Spiral picture. Brightness becomes pitch; these are pixels, not temperatures.” | Keep the NASA image, scan position and generated note together. Leave about four seconds without speech. Capture 02; this is a live pixel scan, not NASA’s original soundtrack. |
| 17–33 s | **Flip the record** to B; enable **Hear Data Only**, then explicitly press **Play record**. | “Flip it. Now twelve selected January temperature anomalies set pitch, relative to the 1951–1980 January average. Hear Data Only removes accompaniment.” | Hold the chart, unit, note and source label; allow a short unvoiced excerpt. Capture 03. Flip and changing Hear Data Only stop the preceding audio. Do not narrate the short excerpt as the entire history. |
| 33–47 s | Flip to A; **Play with Earth**; move across the image and pause at a few positions. | “Back to the picture. Move across it: the same pixel rules, played by you.” | Show pointer movement, scan line and changing note in one shot. Leave room for three separated notes. Capture 04. A keyboard-slider alternative is equally valid. |
| 47–63 s | Flip to B; **Read the grooves**; move **Inspect sector (silent)** to 12/12. | “The groove makes the input visible. January 2025: plus 1.37 degrees. Its fixed band maps to A4, 440 hertz. | 63–80 s | **Mystery Record** → **Play mystery**; listen briefly, then **Reveal source**, without requiring a guess. | “A final mystery: listen, then reveal the source. Your guess is a hunch, not scientific proof. The reveal explains what made the sound.” | Keep the sleeve concealed until Reveal; show the actual random source and its pixel/measurement label. | 80–85 s | Stop any sound; hold the revealed source and **Open full record**. | “See the source. Hear the pattern. Explore.” | Finish on the actual app. An edited end credit may identify TerraJuke / BoboMimi and the sources; it is not another application screen. |

Preserve the short quiet gaps. The default pixel sequence includes repeated notes and dark strips; do not replace its audio or claim every movement changes pitch. In the fixed snapshot, existing strip 8 maps to F3 (174.61 Hz) and strip 15 to C4 (261.63 Hz). These are useful rehearsal anchors, not a new mapping or a promise for other images. Drag naturally; the original slider/Arrow keys are the alternative. Do not describe a sequence of keyboard commands as a touch-drag test.

## What stays optional

- **Looper:** a three-note Record → Finish recording → Replay take is a strong audience-participation follow-up after the main demo. It is event recording, not a microphone recording. It competes with Grooveprint explanation for the short film's attention; leave it out of the default 85 seconds.
- **Your Record:** a personal gradient image can replace the 17-second Mystery segment, not be added to it. Choose an existing synthetic fixture; show Play/Scratch and the local-only disclaimer. Say “your pixels,” not NASA data. Avoid a live file-picker search in a short stage demonstration; if setup is edited out, identify the jump. Changing/removing the image clears the old take.
- **Other records, full mapping details, Create, data exports, Legacy, the globe and optional 3D:** offer during questions. They do not strengthen the core pixels-versus-measurements explanation enough to justify another scene change. All remain available.
- **60-second cut:** 0–4 select; 4–12 A; 12–24 B; 24–34 Scratch; 34–47 groove; 47–57 Mystery; 57–60 close. Shorten narration/excerpts; retain the scientific distinction and baseline. Do not run the full script faster.
- **90-second version:** spend the extra five seconds on unvoiced audio or the selected-value explanation, rather than another feature. Do not claim a full 25-second measurement performance unless it actually plays.

## Capture and presentation plan

Capture the exact research candidate locally. Production `bee2801` does not contain this stack; do not film production and imply it does. A single screen operator is sufficient; the other team member can narrate and observe. No new UI, package or service is needed.

1. Prepare Home in English, Night Observatory, default 2D, normal browser zoom; preload the existing assets and confirm Play is enabled. Use a deliberate user gesture for every audio start. Keep native Stop available. Test the chosen output/capture route separately: system-audio capture is not assumed to work merely because the app plays.
2. For the intended film, capture at 1920 × 1080 if the recorder supports it; verify framing at that exact size before recording. Current research stills are **1440 × 900 Chromium** and **390 × 844 Chromium emulation**, not a 1080p film or physical phone recording. Frame the real UI, not a recreated image of it.
3. Preserve the source visual, baseline, numeric readout, note and input label whenever making a scientific claim. A slow crop/zoom in the edited video may aid legibility; never recolor, replace or retime a graph while calling it a continuous synchronized take. Disclose cuts, avoid speeding up data passages, and do not paste an unrelated soundtrack over live controls.
4. Keep the same comfortable output level between A/B; record narration separately if needed so it does not mask the notes. Listen back with a real reviewer before making balance or quality claims. Captions should carry “pixels, not temperatures,” the baseline, and “order, not elapsed years.” Do not rely on green/amber alone.
5. Prefer continuous actions within each section, with enough quiet time to hear cause and effect. Mystery can reveal any of the four curated records. Use its actual provider/mapping; if Moving Earth appears, retain the attributed-mirror label and explain a still frame when motion is off. Do not reroll secretly to make a staged “guess” appear successful.
6. Respect reduced motion. Current audit captures used reduced motion and therefore a still Moving Earth frame; the generated audio remained interactive. A keyboard-only demonstration uses the original range controls. A phone insert demonstrates layout only unless actual touch/audio/device evidence is recorded.
7. Recovery: Stop, wait for Ready and explicitly retry. A tab switch or leaving Home can cancel playback. If an asset fails, use a loaded existing record and name the changed source; if capture audio fails, retain the visual walkthrough and label it silent. Do not simulate successful audio or manufacture a live interaction.

## Scientific storytelling assessment

| Existing surface | Assessment and concise presenter explanation | Limit to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Climate A: picture + “Brightness → pitch. Not temperature.” | Strong direct cause/effect. “We hear the displayed picture, including its text and background.” The existing 384px analysis copy becomes 48 vertical strips; mean displayed-image luma selects a D-minor pentatonic pitch with fixed 0–1 bounds. RGB shapes harmonics and horizontal position sets pan. | It is an sRGB luma estimate, not calibrated radiance, a temperature retrieval or a month-by-month trace of the spiral curve. Different brightnesses may share a note. The NASA overview and numeric snapshot are related sources, not matched render/data revisions. |
| Climate B / Temperature Through Time | Strong fixed source-to-note explanation, with chart and source link. “Selected January anomalies set fixed musical bands.” Twelve selected years span 1880–2025; data are frozen at the 18 October 2026 retrieval. | These are neither annual means nor all monthly observations. Ten 0.25°C bands cover −1.00 to +1.50°C relative to the 1951–1980 January mean; C3–A4 is an artistic tuning. The chart uses calendar spacing; playback uses equal event durations. Missing/outside values produce rests. |
| Arctic September | Explicit measurement/provider distinction. “Project-derived September sea-ice extent means set pitch.” It reuses the fixed 3–8 million km² range in 0.5-unit bands and the ten-note profile. | A higher pitch means greater extent under this mapping, not greater warming. It is not an EIC frame. Do not directly compare its pitches to temperature as physical quantities. |
| Moving Earth | The live/still control and attributed-mirror disclaimer are useful. “We scan pixels from an archived exhibit animation.” | Animation time is media time, not an observation timestamp. Motion off uses a still. Neither playback nor the poster is a live satellite feed; exact official mirror bytes/complete production credits remain unverified. |
| Grooveprint | The silent slider/readout makes the input inspectable. A: strip brightness sets continuous groove length. B: the existing note band sets length. The outline marks selection even while paused. | Equal angles represent source order, not equal years; Side A/B lengths do not share a scientific unit. Gaps mean missing/outside, never zero. Grooves do not encode accompaniment. Optional 3D remains illustrative. |
| Hear Data Only / arrangement | “Keep the data melody; remove accompaniment.” The measurement sequence preserves source notes while harmony, drums and embellishments are artistic. | Toggling stops playback; explicitly Play again. It is not available as a universal pixel-audio mute filter. Do not equate note count with observation count while accompaniment is enabled, or claim all dynamics/timing are measurements. |
| Your Record / Mystery | Existing local-only and reveal language supports honest exploration. “Your picture is a temporary pixel instrument”; “Reveal explains this random source.” | Personal pixels are not NASA or Earth measurements. Mystery recognition is not scientific proof or a knowledge score. Current generated timbres are synthesized; do not advertise authentic sape/gong recordings. |

No user-facing copy or audio change was required.
